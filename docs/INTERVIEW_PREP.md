# Interview Preparation Guide
## Digital Brand Growth & AI-Assisted Market Research

This guide explains the project in simple language and prepares you for likely interview questions. Read the main explanation until you can explain the logic without memorizing every sentence.

> **Be transparent:** the repository contains synthetic sample data. Describe it as a portfolio case study / demonstration workflow. Do not say the figures came from Mangiee's actual Instagram account or from verified competitor research. If asked about actual Mangiee work, clearly separate your real social media responsibilities from this illustrative analysis.

## 2. 60–90 second explanation

"This is a digital brand growth and market research portfolio project inspired by the kind of work involved in social media management. I divided it into four parts.

First, I created a sample dataset of 60 Instagram posts across Mangiee and five anonymized competitor labels. I used metrics like reach, impressions, likes, comments, shares, saves and link clicks. I calculated total engagements and engagement rate to compare performance more meaningfully.

Second, I created a competitor benchmarking sheet to compare average reach, impressions, engagement rate and link clicks. The point was to understand that a brand should not be judged only by follower count or likes.

Third, I used ChatGPT to help generate 15 content concepts for different objectives such as awareness, education, trust and engagement. I organized each idea with a format and suggested KPI.

Finally, I created a weighted lead-scoring model using branding needs, online presence and growth signals. This helps decide which potential brands should be researched first.

The data is illustrative, so I am presenting this as a demonstration of my analytical approach, not as actual campaign results."

## 4. Explain the four modules

### Module A — Social media content performance

**What it does:** organizes post-level information and compares how content performs.

**Metrics used:**
- Reach: number of unique accounts that saw the content.
- Impressions: total number of times content was displayed; one account may create multiple impressions.
- Likes: lightweight positive interaction.
- Comments: audience responses or discussion.
- Shares: how often people passed the content to others.
- Saves: how often people saved it for later.
- Link clicks: clicks toward a destination, such as a website or landing page.
- Engagements: likes + comments + shares + saves.
- Engagement rate: engagements divided by reach, multiplied by 100 in this project.

**Why not use likes alone?**
Likes show only one kind of interaction. Comments, shares, saves and clicks provide additional context. The right KPI depends on the campaign objective.

### Module B — Competitor benchmarking

**What it does:** compares Mangiee's sample label with five anonymized competitor placeholders.

**Why do it?**
It helps organize observations about formats, engagement and content approaches. It can reveal questions worth investigating, but it does not prove why a competitor performs well.

**What should real research include?**
Real brand names, public post links, observation dates, comparable time periods, content formats, paid/organic labels where known and consistent metric definitions.

### Module C — AI-assisted campaign ideation

**What it does:** contains 15 ideas for Reels, carousels, static posts and Stories.

**Examples:**
- Product problem-solver Reel
- Educational checklist carousel
- Behind-the-scenes Reel
- Customer preference poll
- Myth-versus-fact carousel
- Ask-me-anything Story
- Product use-case demonstration

**How was AI used?**
ChatGPT was used as a brainstorming assistant to expand possible hooks, formats and campaign angles. Ideas still need human review for product accuracy, audience relevance, brand voice, originality and permissions.

### Module D — Partnership lead prioritization

**What it does:** ranks fictional prospects using three factors:
- Branding needs: 35%
- Online presence: 30%
- Growth signals: 35%

Each factor is scored from 1 to 5. The weighted score converts those inputs into a 0–100 score.

**Why use a score?**
It creates a consistent first-pass ranking instead of selecting prospects randomly. It is only a prioritization aid; the score does not predict revenue or guarantee a response.

## 5. Important formulas to remember

**Total engagements**

`Likes + Comments + Shares + Saves`

**Engagement rate**

`(Total engagements / Reach) × 100`

**Weighted lead score**

`(Branding needs / 5 × 35) + (Online presence / 5 × 30) + (Growth signals / 5 × 35)`

**Lead priority bands**
- High: 75–100
- Medium: 60–74.9
- Low: below 60

### Quick example

Suppose a post has:
- 1,000 likes
- 100 comments
- 150 shares
- 250 saves
- Reach of 20,000

Total engagements = 1,000 + 100 + 150 + 250 = 1,500.

Engagement rate = (1,500 / 20,000) × 100 = **7.5%**.

This is only a calculation example, not a result claimed from a real Mangiee campaign.

## 6. Likely interview questions and speakable answers

### Q1. What is reach and impressions?

"Reach is the number of unique accounts that saw a post. Impressions are the total number of times the post was displayed. If one person sees the same post three times, that can count as one reach and three impressions."

### Q2. What is engagement rate?

"It measures how much people interacted with content compared with the audience reached. In this project, I calculated it by dividing total engagements by reach and multiplying by 100."

### Q3. Why did you divide by reach instead of followers?

"Because I wanted to measure interaction among the accounts that actually saw the post. Follower-based engagement rate is another method, but the denominator must be consistent when comparing posts."

### Q4. Which metric is most important?

"It depends on the objective. For awareness, I would focus on reach and impressions. For engagement, I would look at comments, shares, saves and engagement rate. For traffic, I would focus on link clicks. For business results, I would need conversion and sales data."

### Q5. Does high engagement always mean a successful campaign?

"Not necessarily. A post can get good engagement but generate very few website visits or sales. I would first define the campaign objective and then judge it using the relevant KPI."

### Q6. What is the difference between reach and impressions?

"Reach counts unique accounts, while impressions count total displays. Impressions can be higher because the same person may see the post more than once."

### Q7. What did you learn from competitor benchmarking?

"The main learning is that I should not compare brands using only total reach. I need to look at engagement rate, content format and link clicks together. For actual research, I would also verify the competitor set and compare the same time period."

### Q8. Why did you include AI in this project?

"AI helps me generate and organize content ideas faster. But I would not publish AI output directly. I would check whether the idea fits the brand, audience, product and campaign objective."

### Q9. How would you decide whether a Reel or carousel is better?

"I would compare them using the same objective and a reasonable sample. For Reels, I would also check watch time and completion rate if available. For carousels, I would look at saves, shares and swipe-through. I would not decide based on one post."

### Q10. What is a CTA?

"CTA means call to action. It tells the audience what to do next, such as visit the website, save the post, comment, share or register."

### Q11. What is a content pillar?

"Content pillars are the main themes a brand regularly communicates around, such as education, product information, customer stories, behind-the-scenes content and community engagement."

### Q12. How did you prioritize leads?

"I used three factors: branding needs, online presence and growth signals. I assigned weights of 35%, 30% and 35%, converted the scores into a 100-point scale and used the result to decide which prospects should be researched first."

### Q13. Why did you choose these weights?

"They are a starting assumption for this demonstration, not statistically validated weights. In a real business, I would discuss priorities with the sales or marketing team and later check whether the score is related to actual outreach outcomes."

### Q14. What would you improve in this project?

"I would replace the sample data with verified platform exports or documented public observations, increase the number of posts, separate paid and organic content, add campaign spend and conversion data, and validate the lead-scoring model using actual outreach results."

### Q15. What was the biggest limitation?

"The biggest limitation is that the data is synthetic and the sample is small. So it demonstrates the method, but it cannot support real claims about Mangiee or its competitors."

### Q16. Did this project increase Mangiee's engagement or sales?

"No. This repository is a portfolio demonstration using illustrative data. I have not claimed any actual increase in engagement, sales or revenue from this analysis."

### Q17. How would you improve a low-performing post?

"First I would check the objective and audience. Then I would review the opening hook, creative, caption, format and CTA. I would compare it with similar posts and test one change at a time rather than changing everything together."

### Q18. How would you build a one-week content calendar?

"I would first decide the campaign goal and audience. Then I would select a few content pillars and formats, assign a clear objective to each post, write the hook and CTA, and define the KPI before publishing. After posting, I would review the results and plan the next week."

### Q19. How do you measure brand awareness?

"I would start with reach, impressions, frequency and relevant audience growth. I could also track branded searches or direct traffic if that data is available. I would compare results with a baseline and the campaign period."

### Q20. What is the difference between organic and paid reach?

"Organic reach comes from unpaid distribution, while paid reach comes from advertising spend. They should be separated in analysis because paid promotion can change the results significantly."

## 7. How to explain the Excel/dashboard part

If the interviewer asks about the dashboard, explain the intended structure and only claim features that are actually present in the workbook you can show.

"I would keep the main KPIs at the top, such as average reach, impressions, engagement rate and link clicks. Then I would use charts to compare brands and content formats. Filters for brand, format and theme would help explore the data. The purpose is to make the key patterns easier to understand without manually reading every row."

Before the interview, open the actual workbook and be ready to demonstrate:
1. Which sheet contains raw post-level data.
2. Which formulas calculate engagements and engagement rate.
3. Which PivotTables or summary tables feed the charts, if used.
4. Which filters or slicers work, if present.
5. One observation and the next test you would recommend.

Do not claim to have used PivotTables, slicers, Power Query or advanced Excel features unless you can show and explain them.

## 10. Final reminders before an interview

- Understand reach, impressions, engagement rate, CTA and content pillars.
- Remember the two formulas and lead-score weights.
- Explain why the right KPI depends on the campaign objective.
- Have one example of a content idea and explain its audience, hook, objective and KPI.

- Do not claim revenue, follower growth or engagement improvements that are not verified.
