# Content Strategy Playbook

This playbook turns the project's analysis framework into a practical planning routine.

## 1. Start with the audience and objective

Before writing a post, define:
- Who is the intended audience?
- What problem, question or motivation does the audience have?
- What should the audience understand, feel or do?
- What is the single main message?
- What is the call to action?

Avoid starting with a format or trending audio before knowing the purpose.

## 2. Content pillars

A simple starter mix can include:

| Pillar | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Education | Help the audience make a better decision | Checklist, myth vs fact, beginner guide |
| Product / service | Explain features and use cases | Demo, feature breakdown |
| Trust | Show credibility and transparency | Behind the scenes, approved testimonial |
| Community | Encourage two-way interaction | Poll, Q&A, customer feature |
| Brand story | Communicate values and identity | Founder story, process, milestones |

The mix should be adapted to the actual brand, audience and product category.

## 4. Example concepts from this repository

### Product problem-solver Reel
- Objective: awareness and consideration
- Hook: show a recognizable customer problem immediately
- Main message: demonstrate how the product helps
- CTA: learn more or visit the product page
- KPIs: reach, 3-second views, watch time, link clicks

### Educational checklist carousel
- Objective: education and consideration
- Hook: a useful question or checklist title
- Main message: give practical selection criteria
- CTA: save or share
- KPIs: saves, shares, swipe-through

### Behind-the-scenes Reel
- Objective: trust
- Hook: show a real process or person
- Main message: demonstrate care, process or quality checks
- CTA: ask a question or learn more
- KPIs: watch time, comments, profile visits

### Audience poll Story
- Objective: engagement and audience research
- Hook: a simple either/or question
- Main message: learn audience preferences
- CTA: vote
- KPIs: poll responses, completion rate

## 5. Weekly planning workflow

1. Review the previous period's content and campaign objective.
2. Identify one or two useful observations; do not overreact to a single post.
3. Select content pillars and formats for the next week.
4. Write a brief for every post.
5. Review claims, spelling, visual quality, accessibility and brand voice.
6. Schedule or publish through the approved workflow.
7. Record results after a consistent measurement window.
8. Document one learning and one next test.

## 6. Experiment design

When testing a hook, format or CTA:
- State the hypothesis first.
- Choose one main variable to change.
- Keep audience and timing as comparable as possible.
- Define the primary KPI before publishing.
- Use enough posts or a suitable test period.
- Record external factors such as paid spend or seasonal events.
- Decide whether to repeat, revise or stop based on evidence.

Example hypothesis:
"An educational carousel may generate more saves than a static product announcement because it offers information people may want to revisit."

This is a hypothesis to test, not a proven result from the synthetic sample.

## 8. AI use checklist

Use AI to brainstorm hooks, rewrite drafts, generate content variations and organize ideas. Before publishing:
- Verify all factual and product claims.
- Remove generic or repetitive language.
- Match the brand voice.
- Check copyright, usage rights and attribution.
- Obtain permission for customer images or testimonials.
- Check platform and advertising policies.
- Ensure the CTA and offer are accurate.
- Have a human owner approve the final content.
